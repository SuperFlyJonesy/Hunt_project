Loved Ones & Support Network

Hearing loss does not only affect the individual; it naturally changes how families, partners, children, and close friends interact every day.

With a few thoughtful communication adjustments, you can help your loved one stay fully engaged in conversations, family dinners, and social activities without feeling exhausted or isolated.

Essential Communication Habits
1. Get Attention First

Say their name or tap their arm gently before speaking so they are looking at you when you begin.

2. Face Them Directly in Good Light

Ensure your face is lit and unobstructed. Avoid speaking from another room or turning away while talking.

3. Speak Clearly, Don't Shout

Shouting distorts voice pitch and lip shapes. Speak at a normal cadence with natural pauses.

4. Rephrase Instead of Repeating

If they miss a phrase, try using different words rather than just repeating the same sentence louder.

5. Reduce Background Noise

Mute the TV, step away from noisy appliances, or pick quiet corners when dining out in public venues.

6. Respect Listening Fatigue

Processing sound through hearing loss requires immense mental energy. Allow quiet breaks during long gatherings.

RNID: Communication Tactics for Family
Home Environment & Hearing Aids

Getting used to new hearing aids takes 30 to 60 days of brain adjustment. Here is how family members can help:

  • Practice in Quiet Rooms First: Start with one-on-one conversations at home before tackling noisy social events.
  • Encourage Daily Wear: Help them wear their aids consistently every day so their brain adapts faster.
  • Smart Home Assistive Tech: Consider installing TV streamers, vibrating smoke alarm pads, or flashing doorbells for peace of mind.
  • Offer Practical Accompaniment: Offer to attend NHS audiology appointments together to take notes and ask questions.
