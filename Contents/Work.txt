Supporting colleagues with hearing loss at work
Hearing loss is common at work – around 1 in 8 working‑age adults has some level of hearing loss – but it’s often invisible.
england
+1

With a few simple changes, you can make your workplace more inclusive, reduce stress, and help colleagues with hearing loss to contribute their best.

Why it matters
When hearing loss isn’t recognised or supported, people can miss key information, feel left out of chats and meetings, and become tired from constant listening effort.

+1

Inclusive communication isn’t just “being nice” – it’s part of good teamwork, professional behaviour, and equality under the Equality Act 2010.

Spotting when a colleague might need support
You might notice that a colleague:
hearingaid
+1

Struggles to follow fast group discussions or noisy meetings.

Often asks for repetition or says “sorry, I missed that”.

Avoids social events where sound is chaotic (after‑work drinks, busy cafés).

Watches your lips or face closely when you talk.

These can be signs of hearing loss, not of disinterest or poor performance.
england
+1

Start the conversation in a respectful way
If you want to offer support:
rnid
+1

Choose a private, calm moment – not in front of others.

Focus on solutions, not labels: “Are there any changes that would make meetings easier to follow?”

Avoid assumptions or jokes about “going deaf”; keep the tone professional and kind.

Ask what helps them: lip‑reading, captions, written notes, sitting in a certain place, or using particular tech.

Tech, adjustments and legal rights (UK)
Under the UK Equality Act 2010, many people with hearing loss are protected as disabled and entitled to reasonable adjustments at work.
