The HoH Gold Standard for Venues
We’re creating a simple HoH Gold Standard – a checklist of what makes a venue genuinely accessible for people with hearing loss.

Based on national guidance for hearing loops and deaf‑friendly communication, a Gold Standard venue will:

Hearing loop & telecoil ready

Has a correctly installed hearing loop (or Auracast/other assistive system) in key areas like counters, meeting spaces and performance areas.

An update on Auracast: new audio streaming technology - RNID
Displays clear “T” or hearing loop signage where the system is available.

Providing a hearing loop in your venue or business - RNID
Tests and services the loop at least once a year, and checks it regularly using a loop listener device.

Trained, confident staff

Staff know the loop exists, how to switch it on, and where customers need to stand or sit to use it.

Staff use basic deaf‑aware communication (like Jo’s PACE: Patience, Attention, Clarity, Ear contact).

HoH‑friendly environment

Good lighting on faces (not heavy back‑lighting), so lip‑reading is possible.

Thoughtful control of background noise where practical – not blaring music over conversations.

Clear, readable signage and menus; written back‑up for key information (e.g. specials board, event times).

Clear information for visitors

Website or social media mentions any hearing loop or assistive listening service, and how to access it.

Staff can explain what’s available when someone asks (for example, “We have a loop at the bar and in the main room – just switch your hearing aids to the ‘T’ setting.”).

Venues that meet or work towards this standard can be listed as HoH Gold on our Bristol map and in our vlogs.

Community reviews – building the Bristol HoH Map
With Patreon support, we’ll:

Visit venues across Bristol – bars, cafés, restaurants, community centres, libraries, arts spaces.

Record short vlogs or posts after each visit, rating them against the HoH Gold Standard (for example: Gold, Silver, Needs Work).

Log results on an online Bristol HoH Map, so people can quickly see:

Where loops and assistive systems actually work.

Where staff are deaf‑aware and PACE‑friendly.

Where the environment (noise, lighting, layout) makes life easier or harder for HoH visitors.

Over time, this map becomes a powerful community tool – and a gentle nudge for venues to improve.

Turning insight into a paid service
Once we’ve built up real data from the community, we can offer venues a clear next step:

HoH Gold Standard Audit
A paid, on‑site assessment to show venues exactly what they can do to meet our Gold Standard.

For this service, we would:

Visit the venue with testing tools and a structured checklist based on best‑practice hearing loop and accessibility guidance.

Check the state of any existing loop or assistive system (coverage, signal, signage, staff awareness).

Review noise, lighting, layout, and communication points (reception, bar, tables, stage, noticeboards).

Provide a short, practical report:

What’s already good.

What needs improvement.

Priority actions to reach HoH Gold status.

Venues can then choose to:

Make quick, low‑cost changes themselves (signage, staff training, small layout tweaks).

Bring in specialist installers or suppliers (for example, loop or Auracast providers) to upgrade systems.
