NHS Access in Bristol

FREE FROM NHS

Hearing aids are provided 100% FREE as a long-term loan via UHBW Audiology (St Michael’s, Southmead, and satellite clinics).

Lost/damaged replacement device fee: £95.

build Free Replacement Tubing

Once you have your hearing aid, you do not need to buy replacement tubes. You can email the audiology department to request new tubes, and they will send them to you for free by post.

battery_charging_full Battery Supply

If your hearing aid uses disposable batteries, replacements can be requested via the NHS. If you find it difficult to get batteries from Audiology, you can purchase compatible zinc-air batteries from Boots or Amazon.

🏠 Home Visit Support (HISS Bristol): If you are unable to visit the Audiology clinic for retubing or cleaning due to age or mobility, the Hearing Impaired Support Scheme (HISS Bristol) provides free volunteer home visits for NHS hearing aid users. Contact UHBW Audiology for a HISS referral or visit hissbristol.org.uk ↗.

When you visit the audiologist, they will advise you on what hearing aid will be suited for your personal hearing condition. Not all hearing aids will be suitable for you, so you may find that even though you want a small in-ear hearing aid, they may not fit in your ear and they may not be powerful enough to help you hear better.

Also, when you get your new hearing aid, you may need to ask if you can have the hearing loop (telecoil) function turned on. Some hearing aids may not even have this function available.

Why Do Hearing Aids Cost So Much?

Private prescription hearing aids can range from £500 up to £3,500+ per pair. Many people ask why small ear devices carry such a high price tag compared to consumer electronics:

1. Medical R&D & Miniaturised Chips

Hearing aid manufacturers spend hundreds of millions developing custom microchips that process sound millions of times per second, isolating speech in noisy crowds while consuming tiny battery power.

2. Bundled Clinical Care & Aftercare

The price includes years of professional audiologist care: initial diagnostic tests, Real Ear Measurement (REM) fitting, ear canal impressions, routine fine-tuning, and a 3 to 5-year full warranty.

3. Custom Ear Canal Fitting

Unlike mass-manufactured earbuds, prescription devices are custom moulded and acoustically tuned to match your unique ear canal shape to prevent acoustic feedback whistling.

4. Specialised Low Volume Manufacturing

Smartphones are mass-produced by the tens of millions, spreading R&D costs across huge volumes. Medical hearing aids are produced in much smaller numbers to strict medical regulation standards.

Why Can't You Just Use the New Apple AirPods?

While Apple AirPods Pro 2 feature Over-The-Counter (OTC) hearing aid modes, they cannot replace prescription medical hearing aids:

  • Battery Life: AirPods only last 4 to 6 hours of continuous hearing mode before requiring recharging. Prescription hearing aids run for 16 to 24+ hours all day.
  • Hearing Loss Severity: AirPods are strictly regulated for perceived mild to moderate loss only. They lack power for severe or profound loss or complex high-frequency drops.
  • No Telecoil / Hearing Loop Function: AirPods do not contain a telecoil receiver to connect to induction loops in churches, theatres, buses, or public venues.
  • Ear Canal Occlusion & Custom Fit: Universal silicone tips plug the ear canal (causing a boomy "occlusion effect" on your own voice) and can slip out, whereas hearing aids feature micro-vented custom moulds.
  • No Real Ear Measurement (REM): AirPods rely on a basic self-test on an iPhone. Audiologists use physical probe microphones inside your ear canal to match your unique acoustic resonance.
  • Social & Cultural Etiquette in England: In England, wearing prominent white AirPods while chatting with someone in the office, shop, or pub can look silly or be perceived as rude and anti-social. Medical hearing aids are discreet and recognised as essential accessibility devices.

Knowing how to keep a hearing aid working could be the difference between comfortable hearing and social isolation. Regular maintenance includes:

  • Changing the batteries regularly
  • Keeping earmoulds clean and free of earwax
  • Retubing earmoulds every 4–6 months to keep tubing soft and flexible
  • Replacing thin tubes and dome covers

Universal Hearing Aid Battery Sizes & Colour Tabs

Hearing aid batteries follow a standardised worldwide colour coding system. Match the colour tab on your current battery pack:

Colour Tab Size Number Common Uses & Device Models Avg Lifespan
Yellow Size 10 (PR70) Small In-The-Ear (ITE), Completely-In-Canal (CIC), and compact Receiver-In-Canal (RIC) aids. 3 – 7 Days
Brown Size 312 (PR41) Standard RIC aids and mini BTE aids (e.g. NHS Phonak Bolero M70-M). Most popular size. 5 – 10 Days
Orange Size 13 (PR48) Standard Behind-The-Ear (BTE) aids and high-power hearing instruments. 6 – 14 Days
Blue Size 675 (PR44) Super-Power BTE aids (e.g. NHS Phonak Naida P-70 UP) and Cochlear Implant sound processors. 9 – 20 Days
💡 Pro Tip for Zinc-Air Batteries: After peeling off the sticky colour tab, wait 1 to 2 minutes before placing the battery into your hearing aid. This allows air to activate the zinc chemistry fully for maximum lifespan.
