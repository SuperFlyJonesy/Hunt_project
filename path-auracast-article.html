Testing Tomorrow’s Sound

The Auracast Trial at Bristol Temple Meads

September 2025 marked a major milestone for accessibility in our city, and I had the privilege of being right at the centre of it. The session was expertly run by Jonathan Hoskin, Business Development Manager at Ampetronic Listen Technologies.

In partnership with Severnside Community Rail Partnership, I helped organise a visit to Bristol Temple Meads for a group from Centre for Deaf and Hard of Hearing People. Our mission? To test a groundbreaking new assistive listening technology called Auracast.

I knew that giving people with hearing loss the chance to experience this system firsthand in a challenging, echo-heavy environment like a busy train station would be an incredible opportunity. But getting the right hardware to test the system took a bit of behind-the-scenes manoeuvring.

Sourcing the Right Tech

Before the trip, I reached out to Sarah Swift, the Head of Audiology at St Michael’s Hill. I was looking into upgrading my own hearing aids and wanted to know if I could get a pair equipped with this new Auracast technology through the NHS.

At the time, the technology was still in its infancy. Major providers like Phonak hadn't yet rolled out Auracast capabilities, and it certainly wasn't available on the NHS in the Bristol area. However, Sarah connected me with David from Danalogic, a manufacturer looking to implement Bluetooth LE Audio in their future models.

David generously loaned me a pair of trial hearing aids. Along with an Auracast-enabled smartphone from Jonathan Hoskin to act as an audio assistant, I was finally geared up to test the system in action.

"Experiencing announcements streamed straight into hearing aids without reverberation was an eye-opener for all of us."

The Trial Experience

The feedback from our group was overwhelmingly positive. Bristol Temple Meads has cavernous acoustics that make standard public address announcements difficult to decipher for hearing aid users. Auracast delivered pristine clarity directly to individual devices.

We are excited to continue working alongside regional transport hubs, audiology clinicians, and community partners to accelerate the adoption of Auracast across Bristol and beyond.
