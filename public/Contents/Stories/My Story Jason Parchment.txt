# My Story: Navigating Life with Hearing Loss

I started losing my hearing in my early 30s. Looking back, I believe it was a combination of factors: years of spending time in loud environments like nightclubs and bars, paired with a childhood history of ear infections.

In my early 30s, I was diagnosed with a cholesteatoma—a benign growth behind the eardrum. If left untreated, it can cause significant damage to the inner ear, so I underwent a series of operations on both ears. The surgeries were invasive, challenging, and came with real risks, but they were necessary to clear infections and protect my health. 

### Overcoming the Stigma

Because of the physical changes and the emotional weight of everything I was going through, I refused to even consider a hearing aid until I was 45. For nearly 15 years, I struggled to follow conversations, developed habits to mask my hearing difficulties, and lived in constant fear of embarrassment.

When I finally took the step to try a hearing aid, it truly changed my life. While it took time to overcome the stigma, having a partner who supported me made a world of difference. It taught me that seeking help is a sign of strength, not a weakness.

### Embracing the Technology

Today, I use advanced hearing aids that allow me to hear clearly on both sides, stream music, and answer telephone calls directly. Embracing this technology has given me back a level of independence I didn't think was possible.

A highlight of my journey was getting involved with new assistive technology, like Auracast. Experiencing crisp, clear audio streamed directly in a public setting for the first time was a powerful reminder that we are at a turning point in making public spaces accessible to everyone.

### The Hidden Realities and Finding Balance

Living with hearing loss comes with daily challenges that aren't always obvious. Echoey rooms, busy restaurants, and quiet speakers can make listening exhausting. I’ve learned to manage these challenges by setting boundaries, being honest about my needs, and accepting that it is okay to take a break when listening effort becomes too much.

Taking care of my ears is a daily routine, but it is one I embrace because it allows me to stay connected.

### My Journey Toward Acceptance

For a long time, I didn't want to define myself by my hearing loss. I spent years in denial, trying to hide my difficulties. But coming to terms with my hearing loss has been liberating. I’ve realized that while I’ve lost a sense I once had, the technology I use now "augments" my ability to interact with the world.

For those of you who might be struggling, please know this: you don't have to face this alone. You don't have to figure it all out at once, and it is perfectly okay to take it one day at a time.

My goal now is to use my experience to help others in Bristol—and hopefully beyond—avoid the years of denial I went through and find the support they need. Our hearing journeys are as individual as we are, and that is okay. We are here to support one another, share what works, and move forward together.
