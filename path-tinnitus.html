Clinical Definition & Prevalence

graphic_eq What Tinnitus Is & How It Develops

Tinnitus is the perception of sound (ringing, buzzing, hissing, humming, whooshing, or pulsing) when there is no external acoustic source. Over 7 million adults in the UK (approx. 1 in 7) experience tinnitus.

When sensory hair cells in the cochlea are damaged or under-stimulated, the brain's auditory cortex turns up its internal gain volume, generating phantom signals that the auditory system interprets as sound.

Audiology Management

medical_services Clinical Tinnitus Management Approaches

1. Sound Therapy & Ambient Masking

Using sound generators, white/pink noise, or ocean soundscapes to enrich background acoustics so tinnitus is less prominent.

2. Hearing Aid Fitting & Amplification

Amplifying everyday ambient environmental sounds reduces the relative volume of internal tinnitus signals.

3. Tinnitus Retraining Therapy (TRT) & CBT

Re-classifying the tinnitus sound in the limbic system from a threat to a neutral background signal.
