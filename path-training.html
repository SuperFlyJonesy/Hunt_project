Give your staff the confidence to communicate with people who have hearing loss – whether they’re colleagues, customers, patients, students or visitors.

Our Hearing Loss Initiative training is built and delivered by Jason and our specialist member initiates, drawing on years of community work at Bristol’s Centre for Deaf and Hard of Hearing People and lived HoH experience.

A practical, plain‑English session that shows your team how to:

  • Spot early signs that a customer or colleague might have hearing loss.
  • Use the PACE communication framework (Patience, Attention, Clarity, Ear Contact) effortlessly.
  • Run meetings, interviews, and events that don't exhaust or exclude hard-of-hearing colleagues.
  • Understand assistive technology (Roger On mics, TV streamers, live captioning, and Auracast).

Our sessions center around the PACE principles – simple, memorable habits that transform workplace communication:

  • P – Patience: Give people time to process sound and speech without rushing or interrupting.
  • A – Attention: Ensure you have the person’s attention and face them directly before speaking.
  • C – Clarity: Speak clearly at a natural pace; rephrase instead of just repeating louder.
  • E – Ear Contact: Keep hands, cups, and documents away from your face to enable natural lipreading cues.
  • 1 in 5 adults has hearing loss – including valuable employees and loyal customers.
  • Reduces costly workplace misunderstandings, frustration, and communication fatigue.
  • Fulfills Equality Act 2010 accessibility duties and demonstrates genuine inclusive culture.
  • Improves customer service ratings and accessibility reputation across Bristol.

🏢 On-Site Training in Bristol

Our team and member initiates visit your workplace or venue in the Bristol area. We tailor examples to your setting (front desk, shop floor, clinic, office).

Typical session length is 60–90 minutes, and we can run repeated sessions to cover different teams or shifts.

  • Customer‑facing staff (retail, hospitality, reception, box office, front‑of‑house).
  • Managers and team leaders who run meetings, briefings and 1‑to‑1s.
  • HR, wellbeing and EDI leads who want to strengthen disability inclusion.
  • Any staff member who simply wants to be more confident and respectful when communicating with people who have hearing loss.

You don’t need any prior knowledge. We assume nothing and start from the basics.
