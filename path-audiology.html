Hearing loss is categorized into two primary physiological types:

Sensorineural Hearing Loss

Caused by damage to delicate cochlear hair cells inside the inner ear or the auditory nerve. Permanent, but effectively treated with digital hearing aids and cochlear implants.

Conductive Hearing Loss

Occurs when sound cannot pass efficiently through the outer or middle ear (e.g. earwax blockage, fluid, eardrum perforation, or otosclerosis). Often treatable medically or surgically.

Under Bristol NHS protocols, your hearing prescription should be formally reviewed every 3 years, or sooner if you experience noticeable changes:

When to Contact Audiology Directly:

  • 3 Years Since Last Test: You can contact the hospital audiology department directly for a retest without needing a new GP referral.
  • Sudden Hearing Drop: If you experience sudden hearing loss in one or both ears within 72 hours, seek emergency ENT/A&E attention immediately.
  • Device Malfunction: Physical repair, buzzing, or mould refitting appointments can be booked directly.

NHS hearing aid batteries and replacement tubing are provided completely free of charge to registered patients:

📮 Postal Battery Service

Send your yellow NHS Battery Record Book along with a stamped addressed envelope to your clinic to receive fresh battery packs by post.

🔧 Drop-in Volunteer Clinics
